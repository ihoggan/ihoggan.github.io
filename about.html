About

I build the tool,
not the lecture

Network technician by trade, builder by habit. Everything on this site started because I wanted to understand something and couldn't find a version of it I could touch.

What I do

My day job is IT and network support — the practical end of computing, where things have to actually work on real hardware for real people. That background shapes everything here. I'm less interested in the theory of a subject than in what it does when you poke it, and most of these projects exist because I wanted to see a concept behave rather than read a description of it.

So the neural network playground lets you break the training on purpose, and the case studies show the measurements, including the ones that proved me wrong.

Lately that has meant old hardware. Two 2015 office PCs from the server room at work were going to be scrapped, so I asked if I could have them. One now runs Ubuntu, a local AI model and a set of characters that remember me, with nothing in the cloud. I wanted it because if the internet goes down, I still want a way to look something up, or just play something.

How I work

Slowly, and by measuring. The longest-running thing I've built is a pool physics engine that took seventy revisions, and the useful part wasn't the game — it was learning that almost every confident explanation I had for a bug was wrong, and that the only reliable way to settle an argument is to go and measure the thing.

That project produced a working method I now open every new project with: decide before you build, report numbers instead of adjectives, and break your own tests deliberately to find out whether they'd catch anything. It's written up honestly, including the parts where I was wrong for three revisions running.

Why it's all free

Because the barrier to learning this stuff usually isn't difficulty, it's access — paywalls, sign-ups, courses that want your email before they'll show you anything. Everything on this site runs client-side in your browser, costs nothing, and asks for nothing. If it's useful to you, that's the whole point.
