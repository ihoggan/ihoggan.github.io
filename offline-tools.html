Prepare now, relax later

When the power goes, the mobile network can follow within hours, and with it every app that needs the internet. Some excellent free tools keep working with no signal at all, but only if they're on your phone or laptop before you need them. This is my shortlist: what each one does offline, where to get the real one, what to download in advance, and a simple test that proves it all works.

Before you start

Three rules for downloading

  1. Only from the official source. Every link on this page goes to the tool's own website or the charity or government body that makes it. App stores are full of look-alikes with similar names and icons. Is it the real thing? shows how to check what's actually on a Linux machine; on a phone, check the publisher's name under the app before you install.
  2. Download at home, on Wi-Fi. Some of these files are big. Doing it on mobile data costs money and takes ages.
  3. Check your space first. On a phone, look in Settings for storage. Leave a few gigabytes free so the phone itself keeps working.

None of these tools needs an account, and none of them needs to know who you are.

Part 1

A library in your pocket

Kiwix

Phone · laptop · desktop
What it does
Reads whole websites offline. Download Wikipedia once, and you can look anything up with no signal.
Worth downloading
Wikipedia in your language, the medical edition of Wikipedia, Wikivoyage (travel), and Project Gutenberg (free books).
Size
Varies a lot. The full English Wikipedia with pictures is very large, but text-only and "top articles" versions are made to fit on a phone. Start small.
Get it
kiwix.org, or search "Kiwix" in your app store and check the publisher is Kiwix.

This site works offline too. Use offline explains how to save it, so these guides are still there when the internet isn't.

Part 2

Maps that don't need a signal

CoMaps, or Organic Maps

Phone
What it does
Full maps with search and walking, cycling and driving directions, all worked out on the phone itself.
Worth downloading
Your own county, plus anywhere you travel to regularly: work, family, the route home.
Size
Maps download by region, so you only store the areas you choose.
Get it
CoMaps is a community-run version of Organic Maps that arrived on the app stores in 2025. Either works. Both use OpenStreetMap data.
Try this

Once your county is downloaded, plan the walk home from somewhere you go often. If your phone can do that in airplane mode, it can do it in a power cut.

Part 3

First aid, from people who teach it

British Red Cross first aid apps

Phone
What it does
Step-by-step first aid for over 20 situations. The Red Cross says its general app works without internet. There's a separate app for babies and children.
Worth doing
Open it once and read a few skills now, while you're calm. An app is a reminder, not training.
Get it
redcross.org.uk/first-aid/first-aid-apps. Free, from the British Red Cross.
In an emergency

Call 999 first. If the mobile network is down, 999 calls can still get through on another network's masts, so try anyway.

Part 4

The official word, saved in advance

UK government emergency preparedness pages

Any browser
What it is
The government's own advice on power cuts, flooding, severe weather and more.
Worth doing
Open the pages that matter to you and save them for offline reading, or print them.
Get it
The government's power cut advice, and the rest of the site from there.

And on paper, by the fuse box: 105, the free number for a power cut. When the lights go out explains why it's the number to call.

Part 5

Everyday tools that already work offline

On a laptop or desktop, these free programs need no internet once they're installed:

Four for the laptop

Windows · Mac · Linux
KeePassXC
A password manager that keeps everything in one encrypted file on your own computer. keepassxc.org
LibreOffice
Documents, spreadsheets and presentations. libreoffice.org
Calibre
Organises and reads ebooks, including Project Gutenberg's. calibre-ebook.com
VLC
Plays almost any video or music file you've saved. videolan.org

If you use a password manager that lives online, check whether it keeps a copy on your device. Some do and some don't, and you don't want to find out with no signal.

Part 6 · Optional

Messages with no network at all

Meshtastic

Needs small radios
What it does
Small, cheap radios pair with your phone and pass text messages to each other, hopping from radio to radio, with no mobile network or internet.
In the UK
They use the licence-free 868 MHz band. Range depends on height and buildings: a few miles from a high window, much less at street level.
Worth knowing
It only helps if the people you want to reach have one too. Good for a family or a street, not a replacement for 999.

Part 7

The airplane-mode test

This is the step that matters most. Plenty of people install an offline map, then find out in a power cut that they never actually downloaded the map. So: switch your phone to airplane mode, with Wi-Fi off as well, and try each tool. Tick it off when it works.

Airplane-mode test · ticks stay on this device only

Nothing here is sent anywhere. Your ticks and the date you last tested are kept in this browser on this device, so you can see when you're due to test again.

Every few months

Maps change, Wikipedia changes, and apps update. Every three months or so, on Wi-Fi: update the apps, refresh the map regions and Kiwix files, then run the airplane-mode test again. The box above tells you when it's been more than three months.

What I'd want you to take away

Do it on a calm evening

Before, not during

Offline tools only help if they're downloaded while the internet still works.

The real one only

Official sources, checked publisher. Look-alikes are the easiest way to get caught out.

Prove it

Airplane mode tells you the truth. Test now, refresh every few months.
