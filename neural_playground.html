How to use the playground

What you're looking at

  • • A single neuron that predicts a house's price from its size.
  • • Five example houses. Their prices follow a hidden pattern.
  • • Real gradient descent, running in your browser. Nothing is faked.

What to do

  1. 1 Press Start training and watch the neuron's guesses change.
  2. 2 Move the learning rate slider. Reset gives the neuron a fresh random start.
  3. 3 Find the fastest learning rate that still works. Then find where it breaks.

Before you press Start, make a prediction

At the starting learning rate of 0.1, will the neuron find the pattern within 200 epochs? What happens if you make the learning rate five times bigger? Write your guesses down, then test them.

The Neural Network Challenge

The Hidden Pattern

Every price follows a rule of the form price = (a × square feet) + b. The neuron's job is to find a and b. Work them out yourself if you can, then check.

House Price = $200/sqft × Square Feet + $50,000

Understanding the Mathematics

The Prediction Formula

predicted_price =
  price_per_sqft × square_feet +
  base_neighborhood_price

Our neuron learns two numbers: how much each square foot adds to the price, and what the base price is for any house in this neighborhood.

How Learning Works

error = actual_price - predicted_price

size = square_feet ÷ 1000

price_per_1000sqft +=
  learning_rate × error × size

base_price +=
  learning_rate × error

Each epoch, the neuron averages these corrections over all five houses. Bigger houses push harder on the price per square foot.

Why divide by 1000? With raw square feet (1,500 rather than 1.5), the price per square foot gets pushed millions of times harder than the base price. Any single learning rate is then far too big for one and far too small for the other: training either explodes or the base price barely moves. Scaling the input so both numbers learn at a similar pace is called feature scaling, and it gets its own page later in this series.

The Learning Process Explained

1. Random Start
The neuron begins with random guesses: $0 to $100 per square foot, and a $0 to $150k base price
2. Calculate Error
Compare its predictions with the actual house prices
3. Adjust Parameters
Nudge both numbers in the direction that shrinks the error
Done experimenting? What was going on

The learning rate is the size of each correction. Too small and the neuron crawls, and the base price is always the slowest number to settle. Too big and each correction overshoots by more than the last, so the guesses swing wider and wider until the numbers run away. The gap between the fastest rate that works and the first one that blows up is much narrower than most people guess.
