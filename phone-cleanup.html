Phone clean-up

Your phone knows where you've been, who you talk to, and what you look at. Over the years it collects apps you forgot, permissions you clicked through, and settings you never chose. This guide walks through eight checks, the same way I cleaned up my own. Each one is look first, then decide. Choose your phone below and the steps change to match.

Showing steps for both. Pick one to hide the other.

Before you start

Two habits for the whole hour

  • Back up first. Nothing here should lose data, but a fresh backup turns any mistake into a shrug. On iPhone: Settings › [your name] › iCloud › iCloud Backup › Back Up Now, or back up to a computer. On Android: Settings › Google › Backup (on Samsung, also Settings › Accounts and backup).
  • Can't find a setting? Menus move between versions, and on Android between makers. Every phone has a search box at the top of Settings. Type the setting's name in bold below and it'll take you there.

Check 1

Up to date

Most attacks on phones use holes that have already been fixed. The fix only helps if it's installed.

Settings › General › Software Update. Install anything waiting, then open Automatic Updates and turn on every option there, including the separate security ones.

Settings › System › Software update (on Samsung, Settings › Software update). Also check Settings › Security & privacy › Updates for the security update and Google Play system update. Note the date of the last security update: if it's more than a year old, your phone may no longer be getting fixes.

Check 2

The lock, and what happens if it's stolen

  • Use a passcode of at least six digits, not four, and not a birthday. Face or fingerprint unlock is fine on top.
  • Turn off things you don't need on the lock screen: replying to messages, the wallet, and control panels can all be used without unlocking.

Check Stolen Device Protection in Settings › Face ID & Passcode. When you're away from familiar places, it asks for your face or fingerprint, not just the passcode, before anyone can change your Apple account or see saved passwords. It's on by default from iOS 26.4, but check it's on. And check Find My is on: Settings › [your name] › Find My.

Search Settings for Theft protection. Turn on the theft-detection lock and the offline-device lock, which lock the phone if someone snatches it and runs, or takes it offline. And check Find My Device is on: search Settings for "Find My Device".

Check 3

Apps you don't use

Every app is something that can be bought, sold, abandoned or turned bad. If you haven't opened it in months, it's still on your phone, still updating, and sometimes still collecting.

Settings › General › iPhone Storage lists every app with Last Used. For anything you don't need, tap it and choose Delete App, or Offload App to remove the app but keep its data.

Settings › Apps › See all apps. Look for Unused apps: Android lists apps you haven't opened for months, and can remove their permissions automatically. For each app you don't need, tap it and choose Uninstall.

Predict first

Before you look: how many apps do you think are on your phone? Count them, then guess how many you opened this month.

Check 4

Who can see what: permissions

Go through location, camera, microphone, photos and contacts. For each app, ask: does this app need this to do its job, for me? A map needs your location while you're using it. A torch app doesn't need it at all.

Settings › Privacy & Security. Open Location Services, then each of Camera, Microphone, Photos and Contacts. Prefer While Using the App to Always, and turn off Precise Location for apps that only need your town. Then open App Privacy Report at the bottom: it shows which apps actually used each permission, and when.

Search Settings for Permission manager (often Settings › Security & privacy › Privacy controls). Go through Location, Camera, Microphone, Contacts and Photos. Prefer Allow only while using the app. Then search for Privacy dashboard: it shows which apps used location, camera and microphone, and when.

Lesson

The dashboard and report show what apps did, not just what they're allowed. An app using the microphone at three in the morning has some explaining to do.

Check 5

Tracking and adverts

Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking. Turn off Allow Apps to Request to Track, and every app is told no without asking. Then Settings › Privacy & Security › Apple Advertising: turn off Personalised Ads.

Search Settings for Ads (often Settings › Security & privacy › Privacy controls › Ads). Choose Delete advertising ID. Apps then can't use that ID to link what you do across apps. Then, in your Google account, review My Ad Center and turn off personalised ads if you'd rather not have them.

Check 6

Things that shouldn't be there

A few settings give an app or an organisation powerful control over your phone. They're normal on a work phone. On a personal phone, you should recognise every one.

Settings › General › VPN & Device Management. Any configuration profile or management you don't recognise is worth questioning. Profiles can route your traffic, add certificates, or control settings. If it's from a school or a job you've left, ask them, then remove it.

Search Settings for Device admin apps. Each one listed can lock or wipe your phone: you should recognise them all (Find My Device is normal). Then search for Install unknown apps: this lists apps allowed to install other apps from outside the Play Store. For most people, that list should be empty or "Not allowed" for everything.

If you find something you can't explain

Don't panic, and don't start deleting things at random. Write down exactly what you found, take a screenshot, then remove it or ask someone you trust. If you think someone has deliberately put something on your phone to watch you, read the next check before you change anything, because removing it can alert them.

Check 7

Who else has access

Shared photo albums, shared locations, a partner's fingerprint added years ago, a tablet still signed in to your account. Most sharing is something you set up and forgot.

Settings › Privacy & Security › Safety Check. Manage Sharing & Access walks through who you share location, photos and notes with, which devices are signed in to your account, and lets you stop each one. It was designed for people who need to cut off access quickly and safely, and it has a Quick Exit button.

Search Settings for Location sharing to see who can see where you are. Then go to myaccount.google.com, then Security, and check Your devices: sign out of any you don't recognise or no longer own. Google's Security Checkup on the same page walks through the rest.

Check 8

The accounts behind the phone

Your phone is only as safe as the account it signs in with. Two things matter most:

  • A strong, unique password on the account your phone signs in with (your Apple Account on an iPhone, your Google account on Android), which you don't use anywhere else.
  • Two-step verification turned on, with up-to-date recovery details, so you can always get back in, and nobody else can.

Going further, across every account you have, is a bigger job: Who holds the keys to your life? walks through it.

What I'd want you to take away

A phone that does what you told it to

Updated and locked

Fixes installed, a real passcode, and theft protection on.

Fewer apps, fewer permissions

Every app earns its place, and only gets what it needs.

Read the reports

The privacy report or dashboard shows what apps actually did. Check it once a month.
